Augustus Shen

Undergraduate Researcher · Foster Lab, USC

I study how molecular motors burn energy to organize living matter. My work sits at the intersection of quantitative cell biology and non‑equilibrium physics — using kinesin–microtubule systems to ask how microscopic energy dissipation gives rise to large‑scale order.

About

I’m a B.S. candidate in Quantitative Biology (minor in Chemistry) at the USC Dornsife College of Letters, Arts and Sciences, graduating in May 2028. Since January 2025 I’ve been a researcher in the Foster Lab, where I build and characterize reconstituted cytoskeletal systems to understand the physical bioenergetics of active matter.

I like problems that live between the wet bench and the laptop: cloning a motor protein in the morning, then modeling its collective behavior in the afternoon. This site is the starting point of that journey — a place to keep my research, reading notes, and progress in the open.

Research Interests

  • Active cytoskeletal matter

    How kinesin motors and microtubules self‑organize into contracting, flowing networks far from equilibrium.

  • Physical bioenergetics

    Linking molecular‑scale ATP consumption to mesoscale structural order using NADH‑coupled assays and LC‑PolScope imaging.

  • Stochastic transport modeling

    Gillespie / SSA simulations of motor traffic on 1D lattices — crowding, jams, and plus‑end accumulation.

  • In vitro reconstitution

    From plasmid design and baculovirus expression to protein purification and quantitative confocal microscopy.
